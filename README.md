## Current research: many-body spectra and geodesic nets

The objective is a mathematical trace correspondence between Laplace-type
operators and geodesic nets in Euclidean and hyperbolic domains. The operator
should be free between geometric nodes; a physical interpretation is not needed.
The node law and its exact spectral contribution remain to be identified.

Current evidence audit, formal baselines, and limitations (no general conjecture):

https://liuyao12.github.io/spectral-geometry/net-evidence.html

The free many-particle trace is determined by one-particle traces and permutation
cycles on any compact Euclidean or hyperbolic domain. It does not automatically
assign a branching node law. A previous rectangle contact-insertion identity is
retained at `docs/many-body-nets.html`; its crossing net alone does not distinguish
an interaction from independent trajectories.

An explicitly coupled smooth-kernel toy operator is tested as a control, outside
the potential-free target. Its trivalent graph sums are compared with spectral
coefficients and full finite matrices. It shows how adding a kernel assigns
weights and incidence, but does not establish the desired pure-Laplacian result.

The initial one-dimensional circle calculations are archived at
`docs/many-body-circle.html`; they did not test the intended planar net geometry.

To reproduce all numerical results and figures (Python 3.12 or newer):

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r research-requirements.txt
python3 scripts/planar_net_trace.py
python3 scripts/trivalent_toy.py
```

Results are written to `docs/data/planar-nets/` and `docs/data/trivalent-toy/`. Numerical discrepancies and
truncation errors are recorded separately from proved identities. Previous
geometry galleries are preserved below as background.

## Earlier geometric databases

The original direction was a database for **spectral geometry**, studying how
the geometry of a Riemannian manifold (with boundary) relates to its spectrum

* of eigenvalues of the Laplacian, with either Dirichlet or Neumann (or mixed) boundary condition
* of lengths of closed geodesics or billiard trajectories
* or variations thereof

specifically what is known about individual eigenvalues or lengths (rational or algebraic), or collectively as a set. Inspired by and modeled on the databases of integer sequences and of L-functions and modular forms.

To rebuild the local compact hyperbolic surface datasets:

```bash
python3 scripts/generate_hyperbolic_octagon_geodesics.py --all
```

This enumerates primitive cyclically reduced words in the opposite-side pairing
group for compact regular `4g`-gon models in genera 2 through 5, then condenses
geodesics related by regular-polygon symmetries and records their multiplicity.

To refresh the published ordinary billiard inventory after a search update, run:

```bash
python3 -m pip install --user z3-solver
python3 scripts/run_tetra_exhaustive_inventory.py --max-period 40
```

This writes a compact exact source such as
`data/tetra_exhaustive_period40.json`, keeps a large resumable local checkpoint
next to it, and rebuilds `docs/data/tetra/billiards_inventory.json`.

To rebuild the static inventory directly from existing exact/exploratory
sources:

```bash
python3 scripts/update_tetra_billiards_inventory.py \
  --source data/tetra_exhaustive_period40.json \
  --source data/tetra_exploratory_paths.json \
  --out docs/data/tetra/billiards_inventory.json
```

The generated tetrahedron billiards inventory currently records ordinary paths
checked exhaustively through level 40, plus exactified paths found by the
exploratory shooting search. The published page also loads a companion singular
normal-cone inventory generated by an exhaustive stratum-word search.

To refresh the singular normal-cone inventory:

```bash
python3 tetrahedron_singular_closed_paths.py --max-period 7
```

This enumerates primitive cyclic words in the tetrahedron boundary strata,
solves the convex length problem for each canonical word, verifies the
normal-cone reflection condition, and writes
`docs/data/tetra/singular_normal_cones.json`.

To run another non-exhaustive shooting pass:

```bash
python3 scripts/explore_tetra_billiards.py \
  --trials 1500 \
  --max-bounces 90 \
  --max-period 80 \
  --out data/tetra_exploratory_paths.json

python3 scripts/update_tetra_billiards_inventory.py \
  --source docs/data/tetra/billiards_inventory.json \
  --source data/tetra_exploratory_paths.json \
  --out docs/data/tetra/billiards_inventory.json
```

The exploratory script uses `numpy`; `scipy` enables the optional local
least-squares relaxation, and `sympy` is used by the exact verifier.
